# Momo shorts: the character series (discussion draft)

Status: **proposal for discussion. Nothing here is produced yet.** No video files exist. This replaces the old idea of one generic promo film, and the old slideshow stays retired.

## The idea in one line

There are six short films, each 30–45 seconds long, with six everyday people. Each one has a small weekend problem, finds Momo, and ends up at the same table with the same gang. Every film ends on the same frame: the whole gang laughing over food, with the line *"Make a wish. We'll get your gang ready."*

Why a series instead of one ad:

- Each film speaks to one kind of person (garba lover, office worker, sleepyhead, footballer, someone who wants to try a new thing, sportsperson). People share the one that feels like *them* or their friend.
- The same gang shows up in every film. By the third film, viewers know them. That's how a friend group feels.
- As real members join, the series can grow (see "Your own episode" below).

The motto behind all of it: **screens are everywhere and making friends as an adult is weirdly hard. Momo is the excuse to just go out, have fun and find your people.** Reddit has the conversations, but nobody actually meets. Momo exists so the meetup actually happens.

---

## Look & feel

- **3D animated feature-film look.** Soft, rounded characters with big expressive eyes, warm skin with subsurface light, and golden-hour colour. It should feel like a family animated movie you'd watch on a Sunday.
- **Indian and specific.** Gujarat and Ahmedabad details: chaniya choli, steel tiffin, Activa scooter, pressure-cooker whistle, cutting chai, a Manek Chowk-style night food street, a society badminton court.
- **Filmy nostalgia, not parody.** Small nods everyone gets without needing film knowledge: slow-motion entrances, arms-wide hero pose, the dramatic hair flip, silent-comedy physical gags (fighting a printer, losing a fight with a bedsheet). Use *poses and moods*, never actual actors' likenesses, logos or copyrighted characters.
- **Prompting rule:** describe the style ("3D animated feature film, soft subsurface skin, expressive eyes, warm cinematic lighting"). Don't type studio or franchise names into prompts. Several tools block them, and a public brand shouldn't depend on another studio's identity.
- **Recurring mascot:** a tiny steamed momo with a curl of steam on its head appears in every film. It peeks out of a phone screen, rides a scooter mirror, or winks from a plate. *Name to discuss: "Chhotu", "Bhaap" (steam), or simply "Momo".*

### 1. Dhwani — *Garba ke baad* (after garba)

**Story beats (about 35 s):**
1. Navratri night. Dhwani spins in slow motion in a mirror-work chaniya choli, with dandiya sticks crossing and lights everywhere.
2. 1:07 a.m. Music stops. Her stomach growls loudly, like a dhol beat.
3. On her scooter seat she opens Momo, and the little momo mascot pops up: *"post-garba khau gang, 5 log, Law Garden, abhi."*
4. Cut to a night food street. Five tired, glittery garba people share steamed momos and sev puri, still half-dancing.
5. End frame: the gang. *"Make a wish. We'll get your gang ready."*

**Character sheet prompt:**
> Character turnaround sheet, 3D animated feature-film style. Dhwani is a 24-year-old Gujarati woman with warm brown skin, big expressive eyes, a long black braid with a gajra and a small bindi. She wears a vibrant mirror-work chaniya choli in magenta, orange and teal, with oxidised silver jhumkas and bangles, and carries two decorated dandiya sticks. Show front, three-quarter, side and back views plus 6 expressions: joyful spin, exhausted, hungry, laughing, surprised, eating happily. Soft studio lighting on a neutral background, consistent proportions.

**Shot prompt (hero shot):**
> 3D animated feature film. Navratri garba night in Ahmedabad: a huge circle of dancers under strings of warm lights. Dhwani (reference) spins in slow motion, her mirror-work skirt flaring and glitter catching the light. Camera orbits low around her. Dhol and shehnai music, with a crowd cheering in the background. Joyful, festive, cinematic shallow depth of field.

### 2. Rohan — *Weekend refresh*

**Story beats:**
1. A grey office. Rohan types with three monitors and five chai cups around him. The printer jams and he wrestles it in silent-comedy style.
2. Friday 7 p.m. The laptop lid closes in slow motion with a heavenly choir. His tie flies off like a cape.
3. He opens Momo: *"Saturday: momos + board games, 6 people, public café."*
4. Saturday. Rohan, who never talks, is shouting "UNO!" and the whole table bursts out laughing.
5. End frame.

**Character prompt:**
> Character turnaround sheet, 3D animated feature-film style. Rohan is a 28-year-old Indian office worker who is slightly lanky, with messy hair, rectangular glasses, a loose tie and an ID lanyard. He wears a pale blue formal shirt with a pen in the pocket and carries a tiffin bag. Expressions: tired, frustrated with a printer, relieved, shy smile, shouting "UNO!" with joy, laughing.

**Shot prompt (comedy beat):**
> 3D animated feature film. A grey open-plan office in the evening. Rohan (reference) fights a jammed printer with exaggerated silent-movie physical comedy: paper flies everywhere and he gets tangled in a paper streamer. Static wide shot, comic timing, no dialogue, only printer sounds and a tiny frustrated grunt.

### 3. Pari — *Sleeping Pari*

**Story beats:**
1. Morning light on a fairy-tale bed, then the camera reveals a cramped middle-class 2BHK room: a ceiling fan, a steel almirah, a calendar from the local sweet shop.
2. Pari sleeps like a royal, with the bedsheet wrapped like a ballgown and hair across the pillow like a cascade.
3. Mom's voice: *"Pari! Barah baj gaye!"* A pressure-cooker whistle blows like royal trumpets. Pari rolls off the bed, tangled in the sheet.
4. Still in the "gown", she opens Momo: *"Sunday brunch gang, 4 log, CG Road."*
5. Cut to Pari, who now looks fabulous, laughing at brunch with new friends.
6. End frame.

**Character prompt:**
> Character turnaround sheet, 3D animated feature-film style. Pari is a 23-year-old Indian woman with a round face, big sleepy eyes, wildly messy long wavy hair and a small nose stud. Outfit A: an oversized cartoon-print sleep T-shirt with pyjamas. Outfit B: a pastel kurta with jeans and a small sling bag. Expressions: deep blissful sleep, annoyed half-awake, clumsy fall, delighted, laughing at brunch.

**Shot prompt:**
> 3D animated feature film. A small middle-class Indian bedroom at noon, with a ceiling fan, a steel almirah and sunlight through a window grill. Pari (reference) sleeps like a fairy-tale princess, her bedsheet draped like a ballgown. A pressure-cooker whistle sounds like a royal fanfare. She rolls off the bed tangled in the sheet in a clumsy, cute fall. Soft warm light and comic timing.

### 4. Gurpreet "Gary" — *Extra time*

**Story beats:**
1. A Sunday 7 a.m. football turf. Gary, in a striped jersey and patka, bends a free kick in slow motion.
2. After the match the team scatters ("bye bhai, ghar jaana hai"). Gary sits alone, still buzzing with energy.
3. He opens Momo: *"Post-match food gang, 6 log."* He finds the gang, plus two people who want to join next Sunday's match.
4. Momos and lassi. Gary demonstrates the free kick using a momo as the ball. Laughter.
5. End frame.

**Character prompt:**
> Character turnaround sheet, 3D animated feature-film style. Gurpreet ("Gary") is a 26-year-old Sikh man, broad and athletic, with a full well-groomed beard and a saffron patka. He wears an original striped navy-and-maroon football jersey (no real club logos), black shorts and football boots. Expressions: focused, triumphant, a little lonely after the match, big belly laugh, explaining enthusiastically with his hands.

### 5. Sameer — *Make a wish*

This film explains Momo's core feature: **make a wish, and a gang forms around it.**

**Story beats:**
1. Sameer scrolls videos of pottery, a sunrise trek and a stand-up open mic. He texts friends and gets "next time bro" ×3, and the replies stack up like a wall.
2. He opens Momo and types a wish: *"Sunrise trek, Sunday, need 4 people."* He taps "Make a wish", and the little momo mascot blows on the phone like blowing out birthday candles.
3. Notifications fill the screen like shooting stars: *"I'm in."*
4. Sunrise on a hill. Four strangers become a gang, with a flask of chai and a group photo.
5. End frame: *"Make a wish. We'll get your gang ready."*

**Character prompt:**
> Character turnaround sheet, 3D animated feature-film style. Sameer is a 25-year-old Indian man of average build with curly hair, a light stubble and a friendly hopeful face. He wears a mustard hoodie, cargo pants and a small backpack with patches. Expressions: hopeful, disappointed by texts, determined, amazed, joyful at sunrise.

### 6. Meera — *Smash*

**Story beats:**
1. Meera practises badminton alone against a wall in a society court at night.
2. She opens Momo and makes a wish: *"Badminton doubles, weekday evenings."* A gang forms, and weeks of games, laughs and samosas pass in a montage.
3. Tournament day. Nervous, she looks at the empty stands.
4. Then the whole gang walks in with a handmade banner (*"MEERA SMASH!"*) and noise-makers, plus Dhwani, Rohan, Pari, Gary and Sameer from the other films.
5. A slow-motion smash. The gang erupts. End frame.

**Character prompt:**
> Character turnaround sheet, 3D animated feature-film style. Meera is a 24-year-old Indian woman, athletic and petite, with a high ponytail and a sweatband. She wears a teal sports T-shirt and black track shorts and holds a badminton racquet. Expressions: concentrated, lonely, shy, nervous, overjoyed, mid-smash in determination.

### 7. Your episode (as the community grows)

When a real member has been to a few meetups, they can *opt in* to their own 15-second episode:

1. The member picks a hobby or wish (or their story from a real meetup).
2. They can choose a **food avatar** (no photo needed) or give explicit consent to be stylised from a photo. Photo consent must be written, revocable, and deleted on request. Never use someone's likeness without consent, and never for anyone under 18.
3. We generate a character sheet in the same style, place them in a template episode, and they cameo with the default gang.
4. The member approves the final film before anything is posted.

## Tools: what to use (researched October 2026)

| Job | Recommended | Why | Alternatives |
|-----|-------------|-----|--------------|
| Character sheets and key art | **Google Imagen / Gemini image** in Flow, or **Midjourney** | Fast, consistent sheets; Flow can reuse them as "ingredients" | Ideogram, Recraft (vector) |
| Video shots | **Google Veo 3.1 inside Google Flow** | Best overall quality and prompt-following; "Ingredients to Video" accepts up to 3 reference images to lock a character's look; native audio | **Kling 3.0** (cheaper; "Elements" for consistency, good lip-sync, up to 15 s clips), **Runway Gen-4.5** (strong reference controls, editing) |
| Hindi/Gujarati/Hinglish voice | Veo native audio for short lines; **ElevenLabs** for narrator and mom's voice | Natural Indian accents; consistent voices | Kling lip-sync |
| Music | Licensed royalty-free library, or a composer | Clean commercial rights | AI music tools: check commercial licence terms before using |
| Edit, titles, subtitles | **CapCut** or **DaVinci Resolve** | Free or cheap; Reels/Shorts 9:16 export | Premiere |

Note: OpenAI's Sora app and API were discontinued in 2026, so don't plan around Sora.

### Feasibility, honestly

- **What AI does well now:** 5–10 second shots with a consistent character when you supply reference sheets, warm lighting, simple actions, and short lines of dialogue.
- **What still breaks:** the same character drifting between shots (hair, clothes, jewellery), hands holding things (dandiya sticks, racquets, phones), readable text on screens, and exact Indian outfits and props. Plan for **3–6 generations per usable shot**.
- **Workflow that works:** (1) lock each character sheet first, (2) write a 6–8 shot list per film, (3) generate each shot with the same references, (4) put phone-screen UI text in during the edit rather than in the AI shot, (5) add voices and music in the edit.
- **Effort:** roughly 1–2 days per 35-second film for one person once the sheets are locked. Do film 5 (*Make a wish*) first, because it explains the product.
- **Cost:** a paid Flow/Veo plan or a Kling plan for a month should cover a first batch of three films. Check current credit pricing before buying; prices change often.
- **Rights:** use only original characters; no real actors, franchise characters, club logos or brand logos in frame. Keep the prompts and generation records for each shot.

### Order of production (suggested)

1. *Make a wish* (Sameer), which explains the product.
2. *Garba ke baad* (Dhwani): seasonal and very shareable during Navratri.
3. *Sleeping Pari*, the funniest one, made for Reels.
4. *Weekend refresh*, *Extra time*, then *Smash* as the finale where everyone shows up.
