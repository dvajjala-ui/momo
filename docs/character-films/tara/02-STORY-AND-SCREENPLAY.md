# People Still Make an Effort — complete Tara screenplay

**132-second draft edit, 36 shots.** A Sunday late morning becomes late afternoon through clear time jumps. The story is fictional. Do not substitute a real unconfirmed meetup or imply automated delivery/group setup has been implemented.

## Dramatic treatment

Tara is half-asleep in a room she knows by touch. Her first successful action is not finding an app; it is putting her feet on the floor. Opening a window brings a small ordinary pleasure. She can enjoy a day by herself and still want people in her city with whom a plan does not require a long negotiation.

Her phone offers an easy yes before her body is ready for one. A friend shares a small plan, she registers interest and sends “I'm in”, then privately makes the opposite prediction. The contradiction is not a lie with consequences or a moral failing. It is the little distance between being interested and actually deciding to leave the room.

Scrolling compresses several hours. Different poses, water, a blanket and changing daylight keep the day physical. The invitation arrives after that familiar loop. Before opening it, the ordinary to-do list passes across her mind. She has not become incapable of responsibilities; she is making small choices inside a normal weekend.

The surprise is effort. Someone made a little letter, wrote practical information, left room for a no and made her feel welcome without an obligation. We see her eyes before the card, because the object matters through her response. Her line is sincere and small. The payoff of that line is a cut to her hands and feet actually doing something with the feeling.

Getting ready has imperfect comic texture. The sleeve catches, a silly combination is rejected, the cap has a line she can tease. The mirror moment is not a beauty transformation. A passing doubt, a panda-shaped private joke and her own kind answer bring her back into the same body with a little more ease. She leaves because she wants to.

A brief shared laugh is enough to establish the afternoon worked for her. The later voice note is to an old friend, which makes the new experience a memory rather than a brand testimonial. A future opt-in attendee group can extend that memory if presented as a concept. The ending offers possibility, not a guarantee that every interested person receives instant lasting friends.

## Scene 1 — bedroom, Sunday 11:15 / film 00:00–00:14

**TA-L01, under her breath:** “Bas paanch minute aur…” A small exhale belongs after the phrase. The blanket moves because she moves it, not because a new shot resets the prop.

Cut to the bed edge when both feet can land in frame. Knees under hips, palms at the mattress. She leans forward, pushes through hands/feet and stands. **TA-L02:** “Uff… chalo.” This is the first clear whole-body answer to the earlier overly seated staging.

At the window, curtain movement and glass opening may be two simple takes. The iron bars do not slide. Daylight now has a reason to enter. Gentle cool air reaches her hair tips and curtain edge. She lets shoulders lower; one loose strand can be tucked away. **TA-L03:** “Arre… thandi hawa!” Let the line bridge the window and breath shots; do not cram it into a tiny isolated edit.

**Sound:** room tone, cotton, mattress, soft feet, latch/track and breeze. No triumphant music for getting out of bed. **End state:** pane open, curtain aside, lights off, brighter room.
