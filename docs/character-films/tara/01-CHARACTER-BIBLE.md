## Person, circumstances and attitude

Tara works a corporate weekday and rents a room in a flat with roommates. They are out or occupied elsewhere today. Single is biographical context, not a dating premise or explanation for her choices. She has responsibilities, a sense of humor and a wish for more people in her city with whom ordinary plans feel easy.

She talks to herself in small practical fragments. A contradiction can amuse her: she types yes, then privately announces she is surely not going. She does not despise her day at home. Her preparation is self-directed, prompted by appreciating care, not a parent ordering her out or a brand diagnosing her.

Her humor lives in timing: a delayed look at a caught sleeve; a solemn nod that is slightly too serious for a tiny task; the cap phrase landing a beat after she puts it on. Her attitude toward herself is affectionate. A brief mirror doubt resolves with her own reassurance, not a voice telling her that she has become acceptable.

## Adult silhouette and body construction

Slim-to-average adult build with a little natural softness at cheeks, upper arms and hips. Do not enlarge her abdomen/thighs to interpret “chubby”, and do not thin her during dressing. The earlier fuller-body attempt was explicitly corrected. Softness is part of her normal build, not something the story removes.

For a new full-body sheet, a working height around 164 cm can establish furniture/door scale; the number is not an approved measured fact. Use adult limb proportions, natural upper-arm fullness, defined but unexaggerated elbows/knees and a relaxed waist. The selected visual treatment has adult anime stylization; do not impose a childlike enormous head or a fashion-model elongated leg ratio.

At waking, her ribcage is settled, shoulders rounded and weight supported by mattress/pillow. Standing requires feet under knees, torso inclining forward, hands transferring support and hips rising. A more upright back later changes her silhouette without changing her anatomy. Both feet contact the floor before rising; they do not hover or slide while her upper body acts.

## Face structure, features and expression continuity

- **Overall shape:** softly oval face, fuller cheek curve than a sharp fashion V-shape, gentle taper into an adult rounded chin. Avoid making every camera angle a different round/pointed face.
- **Forehead/hairline:** visible forehead between irregular layered front pieces; natural asymmetric part from the selected reference. Do not add a high ceremonial hairline, a perfect straight fringe or a forehead ornament.
- **Brows:** dark, visibly shaped brows with readable thickness and a natural lifted outer line. They can compress/lift for thought or surprise without changing the distance between eyes or inventing permanently angry arches.
- **Eyes:** dark brown irises when open, expressive almond-based outline and adult eyelids. Sleepy lids are heavy, not permanently sad. Wide-eye appreciation increases lid opening and slightly lifts the brows; it does not make the eyeballs bulge or erase the face.
- **Nose:** retain the selected natural bridge/tip and small stud. Keep the stud on the same anatomical side as the locked reference in new angles; a mirror reverses the image consistently rather than moving jewelry to another ear/nose side.
- **Mouth:** natural medium lips with a gently fuller lower shape and small warm lip color. Resting lips are relaxed. Smile grows from corners and cheeks; do not replace it with a permanent toothy grin. Speech uses restrained shapes and rests between phrases.
- **Bindi/ear details:** tiny everyday bindi and small earrings from the existing design, not an elaborate bridal set. Do not add a different large bindi or change metal/jewelry between adjacent takes. Home earrings may be replaced with the selected outing pair through a deliberate cut.
- **Skin:** warm brown complexion stays brown in dim light, warm afternoon light and the postcard reflection. Light changes exposure/color, not ethnicity or a “fairer after makeover” effect. No new freckles, scars, tattoos or beauty marks are invented to distinguish versions.

## Hair construction from every angle

Black layered wolf cut extends below the shoulders, with the established longer tips reaching into the upper-chest silhouette in the C drawing. Keep that actual reference shape rather than recutting it into a chin bob because a short prompt says “wolf cut”. The front has soft asymmetric pieces, not a separate straight fringe glued on top.

Front view: uneven face-framing locks curve around temples/cheeks, with the longer mass continuing below both shoulders. Three-quarter view: nearer-side layers overlap the cheek/neck naturally and the farther-side hair remains visible. Side view: crown volume is soft, not a tall dome; layered ends sit beyond the neck/shoulder. Back view: maintain the same layered length and modest volume, not sudden waist-length straight hair or a new ponytail.

Morning messiness is separated strands and settled pillow shape; it is not an entirely new hairstyle. A brush later aligns some layers while preserving the cut. Cool window air moves the loose tips and a curtain edge first, with a little delayed settle. Do not lift the whole scalp or blow every strand horizontally. Under the denim cap the crown compresses; the side/neck layers still come out naturally. The cap does not erase her hair or create a long ponytail through the back opening.

## Home outfit H1 — construction and wear

Loose faded lavender cotton jersey T-shirt, round crew neck, dropped shoulder seam, roomy short sleeves falling around mid-upper arm. Soft ribbed neck binding, plain front with no slogan/logo, simple sleeve/hem stitching. It hangs and folds with the body rather than tightening into a new fitted crop top. Natural folds gather at waist/hip while seated; sleeve fabric follows the arm when pushing off the bed.

Blue soft sleep/athletic shorts with the selected pale/white edging, comfortable elastic waist and small drawstring detail. Keep the leg opening, piping and length consistent; no switch to underwear, pyjamas or very different shorts at the standing cut. Do not reduce coverage to make the scene more animated. The blanket modestly covers part of torso/lap until moved as actual fabric.

Bare feet indoors. No outdoor shoes appear on the mattress. Home jewelry remains the small reference pieces; no watch, stacked bracelets, necklace or makeup palette is invented in a random shot. A visible accessory needs an approved design and a reason to enter/leave the sequence.

## Eight expression keys

1. Sleep-heavy lids, loose lips, weight still resting into the pillow.
2. Effort face while rising: brief brow compression and exhale, no grimace held forever.
3. Fresh-air relief: eyelids soften, shoulders lower, mouth corner lifts.
4. Plan curiosity: eyes focus, one subtle brow lift, slight lip part.
5. Private contradiction: tiny side smile and a short pause before another scroll.
6. Appreciative wide eyes: lower glow, lips part, small sincere smile after a breath.
7. Little-mission determination: upright back, settled gaze, contained purposeful nod.
8. Mirror kindness/shared laugh: face relaxes into pleasure, no new beauty filter or jawline.

## Five signature poses and action arcs

- Side-lying wake: pillow supports head; hand searches for mattress support before torso lifts.
- Bed-edge stand: feet planted, torso leans over them, palms assist, hips rise, shoulders settle above feet.
- Open-window pause: one hand near sill, weight distributed through feet, loose strand moves then settles.
- Private phone read: gaze leads thumb/hand; wrist relaxed; shoulders/blanket adjust over time.
- Mirror approval to departure: small head tilt, scarf adjustment, approving nod, strap settling and turn toward door.
