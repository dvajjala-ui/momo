# Rohan — story treatment and complete screenplay

**Weekend refresh** · 160-second working main cut · Original fictional adult character · No narrator required.

## Logline and emotional contract

Rohan ends Friday with an ordinary paper/printer problem and a humane little joke, then deliberately puts work away. Saturday is a separate day, not an impossible step through office doors into another morning. A friend has their own plans, so Rohan chooses a clear public board-game-and-food plan, asks a practical question and learns by taking a turn. His paper-aligning habit becomes a gentle recurring visual motif. The payoff is a shared laugh and a forgotten score, not a personality being fixed.

The audience should recognise the tiny decisions before they recognise the product. This is a film about actions, not a demonstration of how many buttons an interface contains. The character enjoys things before Momo appears and retains their own judgment throughout. A clear public plan, practical information and a respectful welcome can open a possibility; a phone tap does not guarantee attendance, friendship or safety. The end offers one concrete shared memory and enough quiet for the viewer to feel it.

## Setting, chronology and stakes

Friday evening office, Friday night home, then a clearly distinct Saturday morning and afternoon public café. Preserve the actual temporal transition rather than walking through an office door directly into another day. The office has everyday duties and kindness as well as a small printer inconvenience. His existing friend has a family plan and remains a friend. The board-game outing is a fictional hosted plan with practical details, not an instant app-created guaranteed group.

A phone screen is never the emotional hero shot. The key reaction is the face deciding, followed by a body action that makes the decision visible. Exact date, venue/address, named host, end time, full cost and practical access requirements remain production placeholders until verified for a real invitation. No invented public members, testimonials, urgency or capacity numbers are needed.

## What the ending does and does not say

The score-paper motif ends with Rohan noticing that he did not need the score to remember the afternoon. He has not become a different person or stopped valuing detail. The optional closing line is his reflection, not a universal claim that everyone will have the same experience. Use a restrained paper/crayon brand card and a clear fictional animated story note. The six-lead ensemble can be connected later, but this film does not force every lead to attend the same event or invent a real gang's existence.

## Optional beats and protected beats

Protected: original everyday context; one clear voluntary interest decision; practical plan information; actual body preparation/journey/arrival; a shared low-pressure interaction; a concrete personal payoff. Optional: the paper-score aside, spoken update to the old friend and reflective closing self-talk. If shortening, remove optional repetition before removing the breath around the central decision or the listening pause that makes the table feel human. A future outside-group invitation needs actual attendance and opt-in, not a silent extra service claim.
