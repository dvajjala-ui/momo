## Rohan's original voice

An adult Indian man around 28, warm mid-low register with a little grain on exhale, clear consonants and an ordinary conversational Hindi/Hinglish accent. Office remarks are slightly quieter because the room is quiet, not because he has a different personality. The game's 'Last card!' has more breath support and energy, not an unrelated deep hero voice. Working pace 105–125 words/minute, short sentences, room for listening. Do not make every line a sigh or mumble.

A brief smile is audible through a released jaw, not an exaggerated happy filter. Keep small inhales separate from the words. Do not synthesize bracketed performance instructions as spoken content. No actor, public figure or real acquaintance is the voice model. A supplied or licensed voice needs the appropriate permission; this file does not invent consent.

## Acting and lip-sync notes

- Establish eye focus and the emotional thought before the first phoneme; do not begin every shot with an immediate open mouth.
- Use a resting mouth for listening. Blinks, breathing and tiny cheek/jaw changes keep a silent face alive without fake speech.
- Preserve closures for B/M/P sounds, restrained lip/teeth contact for F/V where appropriate, rounded shapes for O/U, and an unobtrusive rest shape. These are animation categories, not a claim of a completed phoneme alignment.
- Do not apply a fixed early-mouth offset to every sound. Review actual audio and jaw/lip timing on the chosen take; head movement and cuts can help, but cannot hide a consistently wrong face.
- Long questions may need a little extra room. Audio may bridge a reaction cut if the original speaker is clear; a different person must not silently mouth their words.
- Food, drink and laughter happen in separate practical actions. Swallow/lower the utensil before a scripted line, and set a glass down before a larger laugh.

## Sound layers

Office has soft keyboard/air movement, two paper taps, normal printer mechanics and a drawer/laptop lid. The release is one restrained original chord, not a licensed choir sample. Friday lobby/home and Saturday morning each have distinct room tone. Café uses cards, chair contact, cup/plate sounds and small overlapping human reactions. The score-paper tap recalls the opening; original light plucked/hand-percussion music can warm gradually without narrating that work was bad.

### Mix intentions

Dialogue is the focus when present; lower music rather than raising every word aggressively. Keep sound effects tied to visible contact. Let one short quiet beat precede the most meaningful line or shared laugh. A uniform blast of crowd noise destroys the intimate scale. Do not use recognisable film songs, soundalikes of a particular recording or copied commercial voiceovers. All final music/foley rights must be established before release; no source recording is supplied here.

### Audition checklist

Choose one calm line, one physical-effort/laugh response and one more animated line. Listen for adult age, natural Indian delivery, exact Hindi/Hinglish words, swallowed syllables, unintended ad cadence, harsh sibilants, pauses and consistent speaker identity. Compare alongside a silent visual take. Mark approval only after actual playback, never because a waveform or transcript exists. The voice remains a replaceable part of each batch until approved.
