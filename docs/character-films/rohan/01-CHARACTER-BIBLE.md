# Rohan — original character and wardrobe bible

Working design proposal · 5 November 2026 · Adult, 28 · Film: **Weekend refresh**. The source establishes a somewhat lanky Indian office worker, rectangular glasses, pale-blue shirt/lanyard and a weekend food/card-game connection. The details below make that concept animatable; they are proposals, not an approved image or a real person's biography.

## The person we see through actions

Rohan cares about finishing one small task properly. On Friday he aligns a stack of paper by tapping its lower edge twice; on Saturday he almost does the same to game cards, notices and laughs at himself. He is allowed to enjoy his job and still want the weekend. A delayed printer is a practical nuisance, not evidence that his life is empty. No character calls him a zombie, introvert, workaholic, boring, broken or someone who “never talks.” His voice is already there in quiet office remarks; the game simply gives it another register.

Three identifiers: plain dark rectangular glasses, a longish adult face with a soft jaw, and short black hair that forms one loose wave above his right brow. Work clothes and casual clothes change, but these features do not. His personal motif is **a turn**: the paper turns, the laptop closes, the calendar day changes, a card waits for his turn, and he eventually plays without over-checking the score.

## Adult face construction

Face is a soft rectangle, longer than Dhwani's oval and not a square superhero jaw. Forehead, cheekbones and jaw have a gentle descending width; chin is broad enough to read as adult but rounded at the corners. A visible nose bridge supports the glasses without the frames clipping through skin. Nose tip is softly downward-rounded, neither a pointed beak nor a doll button. His mouth is medium-width, lower lip a little fuller, resting corners level. A left cheek crease appears only when smiling; do not paint a permanent worried furrow.

Dark-brown eyes are medium-sized with slightly deep upper lids. Their scale stays adult behind the glasses; the lenses never enlarge his eyes to cartoon circles. Brows are full, mostly straight, with a small rise near the outer third. When concentrating, one brow lowers a fraction while the other stays neutral. On release, the forehead softens rather than a new face appearing. His ears sit close to the head with a clear helix; the glasses' temples pass above the ears, not through them.

Light, evenly distributed stubble along chin/upper lip is optional in the proposed key, not a dense full beard. Lock it once approved. The glasses are charcoal **#30343A**, rectangular with rounded corners, modest full rims, transparent neutral lenses and no logo. Eye contact remains visible through restrained reflections. The bridge is centred, arms the same thickness from all views, and the pair can slip a few millimetres during laughter but never change frame design.

## Skin, hair and material colour

Skin base **#A66F4A**, shadow **#78513B**, warm side-light **#C48E65**. The cool office palette may reduce warmth in highlights but must not turn him grey or change ethnic appearance. Under café light the same base colour warms naturally. Dark hair **#231C19**, lifted highlights **#4B382C**. Hair is short at nape/sides, about 2–3 cm visually, with a little longer wave on top. Not Sameer's rounded curly silhouette and not a long fringe hiding the eyes.

Front: a loose wave rises above his right brow, natural part slightly left of centre. Three-quarter: near temple reveals a little scalp separation, far wave stays attached to crown. Side: a modest lifted crown, tapered sideburn meeting light stubble, no sudden undercut. Back: simple tapered nape, not a sharp shaved graphic. A habitual ruffle can disturb the top wave for one scene; it should not switch the part or increase hair length. No glossy solid hair helmet.

## Body and movement

Working scale **176 cm**, approximately **7 adult head units** in the chosen stylisation. Slim-to-average, slightly long torso and forearms, natural shoulders and waist, adult hands with visible knuckle planes. This is neither a stick figure nor a body-comparison joke. His seated office posture curves forward during focus, but his skeleton remains capable and comfortable. The film does not “fix” his body by making shoulders broader or waist smaller.

Standing starts from planted feet and a hand briefly touching the desk edge if needed. Reaching for paper uses elbow/shoulder movement, not a stretching arm. Carrying the tiffin makes the bag pull slightly downward on the strap. During laughter, he sets the card/food down, exhales, shifts back against the chair and lets the glasses slip a little. Feet remain on the floor; the café chair does not tip dangerously. He gestures with one hand at a time, often a small palm-up question rather than wide salesman arms.

## Wardrobe A — Friday office

**Shirt:** pale blue **#9CB9CA**, long sleeve, cotton-poplin look, regular fit with room at elbows. One left chest pocket holds a plain dark pen. Standard collar, visible central placket, small matte buttons, sleeve cuffs with one button. At opening both cuffs are down; before printer handling he rolls each once to just above the wrist. Do not cut between rolled and unrolled cuffs without that action or an acknowledged break. Shirt is tucked into charcoal trousers; no transparent stretched fabric.

**Trousers:** charcoal **#424850**, straight leg, plain waistband and one dark belt with a small rectangular brushed-metal buckle. No pinstripes, huge cargo pockets or changing belt. Hem rests on plain dark-brown lace-up shoes **#4C392D**, modest sole and no logo. Dark socks appear only in a seated close view and stay dark.

**Tie:** muted slate **#667786**, plain, loosened at the collar by Friday evening; no corporate pattern. It hangs normally and is taken off at home. A cape-like lift is a subjective original graphic option, not an object that flies around the actual office. This detailed cut uses a real loosen-and-remove gesture rather than magic cloth.

**Lanyard:** charcoal strap with a blank off-white card in a transparent holder, no company name, QR code, personal information or invented job title. It hangs centrally and is placed in the desk drawer before departure. Do not carry it into the café as an accidental permanent accessory. Shirt pen can stay at the office; track it.

## Wardrobe B — Friday home and Saturday transition

A soft stone-grey T-shirt **#C2C0B8**, round neck, short sleeves and dark navy lounge trousers **#2C3445**. Same glasses, same adult face and body. Shoes are set beside the door; feet in simple dark house slippers. The work shirt/tie/lanyard are not magically discarded outside the building. A clear home cut makes the change readable. The room has a simple lamp and desk, not Tara's identical floral room or an expensive bachelor loft.

## Wardrobe C — Saturday outing

An oatmeal cotton T-shirt **#D7CCB7**, a light unbuttoned olive overshirt **#738069** with simple chest pockets, indigo straight jeans **#354D6D**, plain cream low-top shoes **#E5DDCF**, dark socks and a small canvas sling/tote **#9B8B6E**. No tie or lanyard. Sleeves of the overshirt are folded once at the lower forearm; do not copy the Friday cuff state. A plain dark watch on the left wrist can help continuity; it remains the same face/strap and never displays fabricated device notifications.

He chooses this outfit because it is comfortable, not because work clothes made him unattractive. Keep shirt collars, seam/pocket placement and button count broadly consistent. No branded sneakers, luxury bag, hairstyle makeover, skin brightening or body transformation. The source's pale-blue work identity remains recognisable even when his clothing palette warms.

## Props and their locations

- **Laptop:** one plain dark device; screens abstract or turned away. He saves/closes the task, closes the lid and leaves it in the work drawer/bag according to the locked scene. Do not show an unexplained second laptop in the café.
- **Paper stack:** small five-to-eight-sheet stack, not a huge pile. One slightly skewed page becomes the printer gag, later neatly set aside. No readable client information or identifiable letterhead.
- **Printer:** ordinary grey office printer with a simple paper tray. He opens the accessible tray, removes one creased page and closes it. No fingers entering moving rollers, sparks, paper explosion or wrestling a heavy machine.
- **Steel tiffin:** two round stacking containers in a canvas carrier with one strap. He retrieves it at departure and places it on his home counter. It does not become the café food container.
- **Phone:** one plain slim dark device in a brown matte case, blank/away-facing screen unless a later clean insert is composited. No distinctive multi-lens camera module. Work notifications do not compel him to join a plan.
- **Game materials:** original simple colour/number cards and a generic small board if needed. No wager, gambling aesthetic or copied card-brand design. The emphatic line is “Last card!” rather than requiring a trademarked game.
- **Meal:** one small shared momo basket and water/tea glasses at the fictional café. Hands ask/reach naturally; nobody feeds him as a makeover reward. No dialogue with a full mouth.

## Place and day continuity

Friday office is cool late evening. The lobby exit is still Friday dusk/night. His home arrival is Friday night. A clear morning cut or edited calendar cue establishes Saturday; do not have office doors open directly onto Saturday sunlight as if one step changed the date. Saturday plan browsing is at home, then a short urban journey and a public café. Exact venue/date/host/end time/full cost remain placeholders until confirmed; the film never claims a live event or guaranteed turnout.

## Attitude, listening and small gestures

He checks whether his cup is blocking another person's card before moving it. He initially asks one extra rule question, listens to the answer, then tries a move without needing certainty. On a joke he sometimes inhales first, tries to hold a straight face and loses it. He can speak at office and at home; no “silent man finally finds his voice” story. His connection is through a shared game and a willingness to take a turn, not a public performance demanded by strangers.

## Eight expression keys

1. Focus: eyes on task, low brow movement, lips gently closed, no angry scowl.
2. Printer pause: tiny blink, one raised brow and a breath; humorous inconvenience.
3. Friday release: eyelids soften, shoulders lower, mouth corners rise slightly.
4. Saturday consideration: eye shift between phone and the open room, small palm-up thought.
5. Reading the plan: mild interest, one head tilt, no worried permanent brow.
6. Asking rules: open eye contact and small mouth shapes, listening pause after question.
7. Card surprise: fast blink then eyes following the table, a short “Oh!” if voiced.
8. Shared laughter: cheek lift, visible exhale, glasses slip slightly, hand lowers card safely.

## Five signature poses

Paper-stack edge tap; glasses adjusted at the right temple with one finger; standing desk release with a soft shoulder roll; a small palm-up rules question; leaning back in seated laughter with both feet grounded. Use each because the scene calls for it, not as repeated tics every few seconds.
