# Sameer — story and complete screenplay

**Make a wish · 150 seconds / 2:30 · thirty shots · five thirty-second batches.** Fictional long-form production brief, not a finished film. Character-led dialogue; no compulsory narrator.

## Logline and emotional question

A small urban sunrise walk becomes possible when Sameer gives his idea a practical shape and a few other adults choose to join. His usual friends being busy is not personal rejection. An ordinary shared breakfast joke becomes the payoff, rather than a promise that posting always fills a gang.

## Full treatment

### B01 / 00:00–00:30 — A possibility and a normal no

The hook is his interested face rather than a bleak lonely room. A stopped thumb and small private smile tell us that an ordinary possible weekend has caught his imagination. Original clay, city-sunrise and performance-stool glimpses offer ideas, not confirmed experiences. He invites an old friend, whose refusal is brief and perfectly normal. Sameer nods, exhales and settles against the pillow with the phone on his chest. His shoulder, hand and breathing movement keep the quiet beat alive. No stack of cruel rejections or diagnosis is needed; the old friendship remains intact.

### B02 / 00:30–01:00 — Make the idea practical

He looks toward the little sunrise idea and realizes that the plan can still be small and real. Sitting forward is a supported whole-body action with feet meeting the floor. A route doodle and cup on scrap paper reduce the ambition to a short walk and chai, not an expedition. He reads the wish/logistics fields before posting one invitation. Meeting point, public setting, host, time, end and full cost are placeholders until verified. Once he taps, he stops staring at the screen and stages a water bottle near the bag. The thought has one practical physical consequence, with no magical matching effect.

### B03 / 01:00–01:30 — People choose, then details

A later-evening cut explains why information has changed. Someone has freely expressed interest, and Sameer's surprise is small and pleased. A distinct original adult replies casually; this is their choice, not a crowd summoned by software. He confirms practical details before preparing. An affectionate overpacking beat lets him briefly treat a tiny walk as an expedition, then choose only water and essentials. The approved hoodie/cargo look stays constant. A clear overnight cut makes shoe tying, zipping and both backpack straps into manageable details. The batch ends at the doorway, prepared to meet people in the real city.

### B04 / 01:30–02:00 — Hello and a shared pace

The public environment has level paving, railings and a city sound bed. No scenic ridge substitutes for Ahmedabad. Sameer arrives, slows and offers an ordinary hello with space between people. A participant asks whether to begin, and actual feet move at a comfortable pace. Listening, a half-step adjustment and small reactions let the outing feel shared without requiring constant speech. They stop before the participant presents a flask. A cup is parked safely before any broad gesture. One natural small laugh delivers connection before a camera or ending slogan is needed.

### B05 / 02:00–02:30 — One shared memory

Sameer asks about a photo and waits. Someone agrees, and anyone who does not want it can stay outside the image. A small shuffle and blinking laugh are more believable than an instant polished best-friend pose; the phone lowers afterward and nothing is automatically posted. At breakfast he asks whether pottery might be next, and a new acquaintance tells him to finish his chai first. His delight in ideas has become something another person can notice kindly. Keep that lived joke and a small moving table moment. A larger series cameo can be designed later; all six leads need not magically arrive.
