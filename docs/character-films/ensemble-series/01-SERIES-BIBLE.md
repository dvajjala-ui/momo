# Momo to Pizza — Thoda Sa Bahar

**Working series title, draft v1, 5 November 2026.** Eight complete short-episode plans, separate from the six individual origin films. Written fictional stories, not real testimonials or announced events. No media is generated by this pack.

## The show

Six adults have separate lives in Ahmedabad. A small food or activity plan gives two or three of them a reason to meet again. The pleasure is in the awkward pause becoming an ordinary conversation: one chair moved, one wrong photo kept, one person remembering another’s name. A café, bridge, old-city lane or journey changes the action; it is never just a different backdrop for the same phone advertisement.

The audience is an adult who recognizes the effort of getting out after work, introducing themselves, deciding what to order, managing different budgets and finding a free hour. No one has to be lonely, broken or socially incapable to enjoy company. Each person can already make jokes, have friends, work, go home early or change their mind.

## Season structure and emotional progression

1. **E01, Ek Chair Aur:** Tara, Rohan and Dhwani meet at a public café. Names become easier after a wobbling table gives them something concrete to solve together.
2. **E02, Perfect Photo Nahi:** Tara, Gary and Sameer take a walk at Atal Bridge. The photo they keep is the one in which they are responding to each other.
3. **E03, Rasta Dheere Dekho:** Dhwani, Meera and Rohan take a selected old-city walk segment. A self-imposed photo game makes them notice details and each other, without treating residents as scenery.
4. **E04, Baarish Ka Plan B:** Tara, Meera and Rohan move a planned outdoor conversation to a fictional indoor café. A change of plan is discussed and agreed, not a failure somebody must apologize for.
5. **E05, Trip Itni Si:** Sameer, Dhwani and Gary make an Ahmedabad-region day trip to Adalaj. They enjoy a modest outing with deliberate travel, waiting and return beats.
6. **E06, Kuch Bolna Zaroori Hai?:** Rohan and Meera sit on a public riverfront bench. Silence becomes comfortable through one small invitation to notice the view, not forced confession or romance.
7. **E07, Thoda Extra Bana Liya:** Tara, Dhwani and Sameer bring food to a familiar public community table. Uneven homemade portions become a shared arrangement, with dietary preferences and cleanup treated normally.
8. **E08, Agla Saturday?:** All six meet again at a fictional public café over the course of an evening. They choose a possible next activity without needing all six to be available; the season ends with a small real commitment.

E01–E04 happen over several weeks. E04 is a separate monsoon special, explicitly a later-season weather episode; it is not rain appearing in the middle of the same dry winter weekend. E05–E08 resume on subsequent dry, mild-season dates. Editorial chapter cards can make this anthology timing clear. Garba, rain and mild-weather trips are not forced into one literal calendar week. The origin films can be watched separately; a short recap is not required before each episode.

## Acting and camera grammar

Start with something physically happening. Give a performer a task, an interruption, a choice and a result. A smile is a consequence, not the whole action. A two-person exchange has a listener: eyes move, a hand pauses, a breath answers before the mouth does. Let a joke land for half a second before the next line. In most shots the camera is still or makes one slow motivated move; three simultaneous orbits, zooms and cuts do not create character animation.

Use shared eye-height medium shots, restrained close reactions and occasional full-body geography. Keep the conversation axis until a neutral wide establishes a new one. Walking stays along the same screen direction through a sequence. Feet push, contact and lift; clothing follows one beat behind. A wind gust moves hair tips and loose hems consistently while faces remain stable. Do not inflate a character’s head or change height for a gag. A brief drawn reaction accent may live on a separate graphics layer without altering anatomy.

## Relationships, not compulsory pairing

| Pair | Useful contrast in action | A connection to earn | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tara / Rohan | Her dry quick observation; his brief practical check | They can laugh at an imperfect arrangement | Turning her into his therapist |
| Tara / Dhwani | Tara takes a beat; Dhwani offers a concrete choice | Easy invitation without persuasion | A loud friend dragging a reluctant friend |
| Gary / Sameer | Gary moves a group physically; Sameer notices a detail | Shared curiosity can slow their pace | Making the Sikh character a volume gag |
| Dhwani / Meera | Different dance experiences; mutual attention | They ask rather than pretend all dance is the same | Swapping costumes/cultures as interchangeable |
| Rohan / Meera | Both leave room before answering | Silence can be companionable | Default romantic music or confession |
| Tara / Sameer | Her one-line joke; his small practical proposal | A plan can remain modest | Guaranteed instant permanent friendship |

No unrequested romance, jealousy triangle, body insult, parental shame, dialect mockery or cruelty to service staff. Each character sometimes initiates and sometimes listens. Nobody’s job in the show is permanently to rescue another person.

## Recurring details and their limits

- Rohan aligns paper twice in an origin film; later he can choose to leave one corner crooked. Use once in an episode, not every line.
- Tara sometimes notices a cap or reflection before making a choice. Keep her existing face/build; confidence is posture and attention.
- Dhwani can hear a tabletop rhythm but does not dance through every café.
- Gary makes room physically: moving his own bag, turning a chair, slowing his walk. He does not command the group.
- Sameer proposes something small enough to do, then checks a practical detail. His default brow is open and curious.
- Meera notices balance and space but also makes ordinary mistakes and laughs. Every scene does not have to prove artistic excellence.
- Bhaap is an optional original scrapbook-margin doodle on a title/end card. Do not insert a floating mascot into a quiet human conversation.

## Brand presence

Keep Momo to Pizza as the facilitator in the background. A small plan insert or closing scrapbook mark is enough. Nobody recites product features mid-conversation. The food editions stay parallel: a steamed-momo gathering and a pizza-table gathering can have equal warmth. Real UI must follow the current product; a planned attendee-only optional group is not advertised as automatic live WhatsApp functionality.

These are fictional concepts. Before a public campaign, replace plan placeholders with confirmed logistics or remove actionable event claims. No fake testimonials, membership counts, venue partnership claims, pressure or guaranteed-friend slogans. Real locations are background references, not endorsements.
