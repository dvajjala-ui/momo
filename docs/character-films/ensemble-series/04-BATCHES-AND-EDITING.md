# Ensemble production in small replaceable batches

All runtimes are planned edit lengths, not generated clips. Keep each episode as its own project with stable shot and dialogue IDs. Four eight-second shots generally make a 32-second review batch; eighteen-shot episodes use five batches, with the last two shots forming the closing batch. Batch boundaries are editorial review boundaries, not a demand to create one long uninterrupted shot.

## Prepare once, then reuse deliberately

1. The actual selected Tara reference and actual Sameer v2 reference remain available identity sources. Build and review the proposed six-person 2D adaptation lineup before asking for ensemble motion. Other new sheets described in writing do not yet exist.
2. Prepare only the wardrobe states the chosen episode uses. Every sheet includes front/three-quarter/side/back, footwear, bag side, hair arrangement and neutral/speaking/listening/laughing expressions.
3. Prepare a simple location wide and overhead seating/entrance diagram. Lock table/window/door/river positions before closeups; give each recurring prop an owner and start/end state.
4. Assign accepted voice aliases and review short paired dialogue with natural pauses. Use the exact per-episode voice ledger; measure real clips later, then adjust picture timing.
5. First motion trial: one two-person eight-second exchange with a physical handoff. Check stable faces, ownership, eyes, mouth attribution, hand contact and readable body weight. The earlier approval-first workflow remains useful when generation is later requested; this writing task does not trigger generation.

## Per-shot working record

```text
episode: E01
shot: E01-S05
batch: E01-B02
version: v1
cast/wardrobe: [from episode]
input_reference: [real chosen image path, not invented]
start_state: [pose / prop hand / seating / light]
line_ids: [exact ledger IDs]
picture_take: [not generated]
voice_take: [not generated]
end_state: [what the next shot inherits]
accepted: no
review_note: [one observable correction]
```

Use new versions rather than overwriting an accepted take. A face correction can affect every shot featuring that face; a line wording change affects that line’s mouth performance and possibly the adjacent listening reaction, not the whole episode. A location-door change may require all angles in that scene. Record the true dependency, not a promise that every change is isolated to one file.

## Editing and sound

An eight-second picture window can contain a quiet lead-in, one or two short lines and a reaction. It is not eight seconds of continuous speech. Give a long reply another angle/shot instead of forcing rapid delivery. Insert graphics on clean plates; do not let generated phone text determine product copy. Let a footstep, chair scrape, paper tap or cup set-down motivate cuts. Preserve a common room-tone bed through a conversation, change it on actual location cuts and leave jokes a little air.

Music is original/cleared and sparse: a light plucked/percussive phrase for practical comedy, a warmer sustained texture for relief, and silence for E06's important bench pause. No copied film melody, celebrity voice imitation or named-generation product is needed. Dialogue stays clear over fans/rain/traffic; sounds follow material contact. Laughter can continue across a cut but cannot start before the visual joke occurs unless the listener caused it.

## Deliverable layouts

The cinematic master is planned at 16:9 with faces and important props kept inside a central region useful for later vertical reframing. A 9:16 version needs reviewed alternate framing for wide group shots; do not crop off the listening person merely to fit. Keep clean picture, dialogue by character, ambience, object sounds, music and text/subtitles replaceable. Use one plain subtitle style with speaker identification only where genuinely needed; labels do not cover a handoff or food.

## Review one batch at a time

Watch without sound first: can you tell what changed physically and who is reacting? Then listen without picture: is each speaker distinct, are names/place words natural and does the conversation make sense? Watch together: check lips, gaze, timing and props. At batch joins verify garment state, chair order, daylight direction, phone/bag ownership, drink level and finished actions. Keep an accepted end-frame reference for adjoining continuous shots. For a deliberate time jump, record the new day/wardrobe/weather explicitly.
