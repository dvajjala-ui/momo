# Dhwani — story treatment and complete screenplay

**Garba ke baad** · 160-second working main cut · Original fictional adult character · No narrator required.

## Logline and emotional contract

A dance round ends but Dhwani still wants the evening to continue. Her friends' separate departures are ordinary, so a small hunger joke, a clear public food plan and a thoughtful welcome give her a voluntary next step. Packing sticks, settling her gajra and making one practical journey turn a phone tap into action. The shared table offers names, food and a new little rhythm; the ending is a pleasant memory rather than a guaranteed gang.

The audience should recognise the tiny decisions before they recognise the product. This is a film about actions, not a demonstration of how many buttons an interface contains. The character enjoys things before Momo appears and retains their own judgment throughout. A clear public plan, practical information and a respectful welcome can open a possibility; a phone tap does not guarantee attendance, friendship or safety. The end offers one concrete shared memory and enough quiet for the viewer to feel it.

## Setting, chronology and stakes

A continuous fictional after-midnight garba evening in an Ahmedabad-inspired urban setting. Start with an ongoing last dance phrase, not a false claim about a named real ground's schedule. It is warm from dancing and cooler at the edge; lights and surrounding activity remain plausible. The short food journey and public table exist within a fictional host-confirmed plan. Do not label an actual venue as open or available at this hour without confirming it. Her original friends can leave for their own reasons; the new table does not erase them.

A phone screen is never the emotional hero shot. The key reaction is the face deciding, followed by a body action that makes the decision visible. Exact date, venue/address, named host, end time, full cost and practical access requirements remain production placeholders until verified for a real invitation. No invented public members, testimonials, urgency or capacity numbers are needed.

## What the ending does and does not say

The last rhythm is small enough to belong to people at one table. It can finish on her practical bag/feet gesture and a last glance. The optional closing line is her personal feeling that the evening continued; it does not claim a venue, a real meetup or a guaranteed social outcome. Use a restrained paper/crayon brand card and a clear fictional animated story note. The six-lead ensemble can be connected later, but this film does not force every lead to attend the same event or invent a real gang's existence.

## Optional beats and protected beats

Protected: original everyday context; one clear voluntary interest decision; practical plan information; actual body preparation/journey/arrival; a shared low-pressure interaction; a concrete personal payoff. Optional: the fabric-clearance joke, photo and soft closing self-talk. If shortening, remove optional repetition before removing the breath around the central decision or the listening pause that makes the table feel human. A future outside-group invitation needs actual attendance and opt-in, not a silent extra service claim.
