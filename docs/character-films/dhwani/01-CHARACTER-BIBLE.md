# Dhwani — original character and wardrobe bible

Working design proposal · 5 November 2026 · Adult, 24 · Film: **Garba ke baad**. The source establishes a Gujarati woman, garba, a long braid/gajra, bright mirror-work clothing and a food connection afterward. The precise face, measurements and garments below are new production proposals, not an already approved model or a description of a real person.

## What makes her recognisable

Dhwani finishes a dance with her shoulders still remembering the rhythm after the music stops. When thinking, she taps two fingers once against the dandiya pouch, then notices she is doing it and smiles. Hunger becomes one small joke, not her entire identity. She knows what she likes, asks practical questions and can change her mind. She is happy dancing before the app appears; a food plan simply gives the evening another enjoyable chapter. No dialogue calls her “the extrovert,” fearless, lonely or dependent on a gang.

Her three immediate identifiers are a long black braid ending below her shoulder blades, a magenta/teal chaniya choli with restrained original mirror geometry, and a tiny central maroon bindi. They must remain readable without a franchise costume, copied actress or exaggerated regional caricature. Her name in Hindi is **ध्वनि**; exact name text is an edited graphic if needed, never random garment lettering.

## Adult face construction

Use an oval face with a little cheek fullness and a gently tapering, rounded chin; the lower face is neither a pointed heart nor a square block. The cheekbones sit slightly wider than the jaw. In three-quarter view the far cheek narrows naturally, rather than both cheeks remaining symmetrically visible. The forehead is medium height, hairline softly rounded with a small natural unevenness at the temples. The nose has a clear but modest bridge and a softly rounded tip; nostrils widen subtly during an exhale. Do not replace it with a tiny upturned doll nose.

Eyes are medium-large within an adult face, dark brown with a modest warm catchlight. Upper lids have a defined curve; lower lids remain quiet unless she smiles. Keep normal sclera proportions: surprise widens eyelids and lifts brows, not huge white circles. Brows are dark, full through the inner half, taper toward the tail and have a low soft arch. Do not permanently raise them into worry. The mouth has a fuller lower lip and a slightly defined cupid's bow; the resting corners are neutral, not a constant grin. A smile begins unevenly on her right before broadening. Cheek compression and lower-lid lift should accompany genuine laughter.

Ears are natural adult scale and partly visible behind the hair at the temples. Small oxidised-silver jhumkas move on their own joints; they never pass through her jaw or grow larger between shots. No nose ring is required for this proposal. The tiny round maroon bindi sits on the centre forehead, above the brows, and does not become a gemstone or migrate sideways.

## Skin, colour and light

Illustrative base skin key: warm medium-brown **#A56845**, mid-shadow **#744B36**, warm lit plane **#C78D63**, blush accent **#B77560** used sparingly. These are colour-script anchors, not a rule that every shot has the same RGB pixel. Golden practical lights may warm her face while teal ambient night shadows cool it; neither should bleach her skin or turn it orange. The back of hands, elbows, neck and face remain the same person. A little post-dance sheen appears at hairline/temples and fades after wiping; it is not an all-over wet plastic shader.

Use soft stylised 3D volumes, restrained skin texture and readable cloth. Adult proportions and individual Indian facial design matter more than pore detail. Keep smile lines as shallow expression changes, not permanent aging creases. Makeup is light: a little dark lash definition, muted rosy-brown lip colour, no extreme contouring or brightening filter.

## Body, silhouette and balance

Working height reference **164 cm**, approximately **6.8–7 head units** in the chosen stylisation; this is a drafting aid, not an in-story statistic. Average adult build with strong-looking calves from an active hobby, natural waist, comfortable upper arms and thighs. Do not shrink the waist or enlarge hips between dance and eating scenes. Shoulders are a little narrower than the skirt silhouette; hands are adult-sized with four fingers and a thumb. Neck length and collarbone placement remain stable with head turns.

She carries weight through the whole foot, with knees soft after dancing. A turn has anticipation through the feet and torso, a clear pivot and a settled finish; the skirt follows rather than leading the skeleton. When she stops, let the braid and earrings finish their arcs a fraction after the body. At a table she lowers herself into the chair, shifts the skirt clear of the seat and only then relaxes. No permanent model pose, impossible back arch, floating feet or unmotivated body bounce.

## Hair from every view

Black/dark espresso **#201A18**, with warm highlights **#49342A** under festival lights. Centre part, smooth but not helmet-flat scalp hair, a long three-strand braid beginning at the back of the head and ending below the shoulder blades. The braid is not a rope of identical spheres: broad plaits narrow gently toward a dark cloth tie. The proposal uses a small off-white gajra arc at the braid base, not a huge floral crown. A few short temple strands soften her face. Do not introduce a fringe or change the part halfway through the film.

Front: centre part, both brows readable, braid can be moved over her **left shoulder** after dancing. Three-quarter: a visible hairline and correct ear overlap, braid attached behind the skull. Side: volume at crown stays modest, no suddenly oversized bun. Back: part joins braid cleanly, gajra clips into the braid base rather than floating. Record the left-shoulder transfer as an action or deliberate cut; it must not swap shoulders every edit. The gajra is lightly compressed after a long night; DH18 shows one adjustment, not a replacement flower garland.

## Main wardrobe: one continuous festival outfit

**Choli:** magenta **#B82C68** cotton-silk-look blouse with a simple round neck, short sleeves ending above mid-upper arm and a secure back closure. An original narrow teal piping follows neckline and sleeve edge. Mirror pieces are small circles/diamonds inside stitched gold-thread borders, spaced rather than covering every surface. Do not render real brand marks, religious symbols used as decoration, copied designer embroidery or a drastically deep neckline. Blouse hem meets the skirt waistband naturally; ordinary comfortable framing.

**Chaniya/skirt:** ankle-length teal **#177E80**, full enough for a readable flare, with a broad lower magenta border and two thin muted-gold bands **#BF9B56**. The waistband is secure, same height in every shot. Original repeating stitched motifs are broad and simplified so they can survive motion. The lower hem stays a few centimetres above the ground with her flats on; she lifts only a small side section while stepping around a chair. No transparent cloth or skirt becoming shorter during sitting.

**Dupatta:** burnt-orange **#C86B37**, light but opaque, draped from her right shoulder across the front and secured at the shoulder with a hidden plain pin. It is not a long loose ribbon near wheels. One end falls behind her left side, ending above knee level. A narrow gold edge catches light; pattern stays simple. She can gather the end over her forearm while sitting. Do not redraw the drape as a sari, move the pin to another shoulder or allow fabric to intersect arms/neck.

**Footwear:** plain tan closed-toe flat juttis **#9C6B43**, slightly worn at toe edges, no logos. The reference keys establish whether footwear stays on at the chosen fictional venue; no blanket claim about all garba grounds. This film keeps the same flats visible in walking scenes and does not cut randomly between bare feet and shoes.

**Jewellery:** one pair of modest silver jhumkas, two thin oxidised bangles on the right wrist and one plain silver bangle on the left. No heavy necklace in this working design, so neckline and breathing stay clear. Her tiny bindi is not jewellery. Jhumkas and bangles need finite hinge motion; they may softly clink but not swing like pendulums without body movement.

## Props and ownership ledger

- **Dandiya pair:** two short original sticks, magenta/teal wraps and small gold bands, rounded ends, no bells. She holds one per hand during dance, then places both into a cloth sleeve together. Neither is a spear or wand. The pair stays in her bag sleeve after packing.
- **Tote:** small dark-indigo cloth tote, one broad strap, internal zipped pocket for phone and keys, external stitched sleeve for the stick pair. Worn over the left shoulder when walking, placed beside her chair or on a spare hook at the table. No luxury-logo hardware.
- **Phone:** one plain dark slim device in a matte indigo case. Keep screen turned away or black for later compositing. Do not invent a recognisable camera cluster. DH09 establishes pickup; DH17 puts it into the tote; DH29 can retrieve it for an opt-in group photo; record each transfer.
- **Steel bottle:** a small plain water bottle, originally in tote, used only where shown. Do not replace it with a glass while she is walking.
- **Meal:** original steamed momos, a small sauce bowl and water at the fictional table. She does not speak with a mouth full of food. Steam is a quiet food detail, not a face-obscuring cloud.
- **Transport:** she may stand beside a parked plain scooter while reading the plan. No moving-scooter stunt or loose-skirt riding is required. The short journey is an ordinary walking/arrival ellipsis, with actual logistics supplied by a real plan before public use.

## Attitude and micro-behaviour

When asked something, she looks at the person before answering rather than making every response toward camera. She uses the back of a wrist lightly at the forehead, remembers the bangles, and reaches for a cloth instead: a small practical joke. Before joining the table she asks where to leave the sticks. When another person is speaking she has listening motion—breath, one nod, a small eye shift—not continuous animated talking. At the food table she makes a rhythm with two fingertips, then deliberately stops so another person can speak. Her pleasure comes from participation, not from performing for an approving crowd.

## Eight expression keys

1. **Enjoying a turn:** eyes tracking the circle, closed small smile, brows relaxed; not a giant advertising grin.
2. **Catching breath:** mouth slightly open, nostril/shoulder response, gaze steady; no distress performance.
3. **Hunger joke:** downward eye glance, one raised brow, lips pressed to contain a laugh.
4. **Reading logistics:** lids a little lowered, brows gathering mildly, mouth still; thoughtful rather than worried.
5. **Choosing the plan:** a small right-corner smile, chin lifts, breath becomes a little quicker.
6. **First hello:** eye contact, open jaw for one brief greeting, shoulders neither hunched nor theatrically spread.
7. **Sauce surprise:** lips part, tiny blink and exhale, eyes stay normal-sized; no pain caricature.
8. **Shared laughter:** cheek/lower-lid compression, brief head tip, clear exhale, hand safely lowers food first.

## Five signature poses

Grounded finishing turn; dandiya pair held low after dancing; one hand steadying the tote while the other checks the phone; settled seated pose with dupatta clear of the plate; fingertip rhythm offered to the table then hands resting. These are functional actions, not compulsory repeated poses in every shot.
