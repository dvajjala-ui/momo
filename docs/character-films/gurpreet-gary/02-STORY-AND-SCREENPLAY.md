# Gurpreet “Gary” — story and complete screenplay

**Extra time · 150 seconds / 2:30 · thirty shots · five thirty-second batches.** Fictional long-form production brief, not a finished film. Character-led dialogue; no compulsory narrator.

## Logline and emotional question

When a Sunday football session ends before Gary's desire to chat does, he turns a normal rest-and-food break into a small shared plan. A miniature napkin tactics joke becomes a real conversation, and a freely offered later game lets connection continue without promising a guaranteed full team.

## Full treatment

### B01 / 00:00–00:30 — The session ends, not the mood

Begin with a simple readable football action: attention, planted foot, controlled pass and a small result. The camera can cut around difficult ball contact rather than demand an elaborate orbit around every limb. Gary enjoys the game; his warm face is not a fixed scowl. A last exchange of play ends and the body slows with real breathing. Old teammates collect their bags because they have other responsibilities. Their departure is ordinary, not a betrayal. Gary calls a friendly response and returns a wave, leaving space for the sound of the pitch to settle. The hook is that he still has a little conversational energy, not that sport must fix a personal flaw.

### B02 / 00:30–01:00 — A rest break becomes a plan

He rests before doing anything else. Water, a cap carefully put back and a towel used at the neck make his body feel like it has just played. Hunger arrives as a small personal joke rather than a giant insulting stomach effect. The phone becomes one practical route to a food-and-chat plan. He reads public logistics and makes a voluntary choice; the film does not need a magical avalanche of signups. The footwear change is a real cut with turf shoes secured in a bag and plain trainers on his feet. Keep headwear stable and kit consistent. A short elapsed-time cue permits a plausible move from pitch to public food spot.

### B03 / 01:00–01:30 — Make space at the table

Gary arrives in normal motion with his bag rather than appearing instantly after a phone tap. A small greeting and chair move offer another adult room to sit. He does not presume a hug or ask someone to prove they are a football fan. He asks a genuine question; a participant says they play a little. His reply is a casual invitation, subject to real plan/capacity confirmation, not an app granting team membership. Food is set on the table with ownership and contact clear. When another person speaks, Gary actually listens: mouth rests, eyes attend, gestures stop. One shared little laugh can grow from a simple game or a missed conversational beat.

### B04 / 01:30–02:00 — A tiny play, a shared joke

His enthusiasm shrinks a football tactic down to a tiny folded napkin on the table. One finger moves it a few centimeters; cups remain safely outside that motion. A participant asks whether their job is to save the chai, and Gary recognizes the better joke immediately. The table laugh is shared instead of performed for the brand. He then offers next Sunday with permission to decline. The answer is practical: confirm the plan first. That sentence matters because the story should show choice and logistics, not imply that one social morning guarantees a permanent team. Hold the pauses and listening reactions rather than turning the exchange into a fast slogan.

### B05 / 02:00–02:30 — Another Sunday, one more pass

A visible Next Sunday edit and clean kit establish elapsed time. Gary meets a couple of familiar faces, not a huge team delivered by software. Another simple pass is enough to show the activity continuing. A short game call lands with the same vocal identity, then they take a normal water break before any chai. He notices the extra time of talking as well as playing; his last line can sound like a real person remembering a morning rather than a marketing pitch. The ending can remain at the bench or make a deliberate short food-table return. The point is continuity of a small connection, with room for everyone to decide their own next outing.

## Timeline and unfilled real-world details

Later that morning: a fictional public food stop. A deliberate Next Sunday cue establishes the coda's second day, clean washed kit and separately confirmed casual game. Exact turf/eatery, access, host, date, cost, end time, capacity and transport remain placeholders until real confirmation.
