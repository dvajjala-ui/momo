# Gurpreet “Gary” — character and visual bible

**Film:** Extra time · **Age:** 26 · **Status:** detailed original design proposal based on the existing story description. No approved new Gary model sheet has been supplied. The specifications below are a coherent working design to review before rendering repeated shots; they are not claims about a real person's appearance.

## Identity and silhouette

Gary is a Sikh adult football player, broad through shoulders and upper back, with strong thighs and calves that plausibly support kicking, deceleration and a quick change of direction. Athletic does not mean an exaggerated bodybuilder torso. Give him an ordinary soft abdominal transition under the shirt, real neck thickness and knees/ankles that bend naturally. He is visually broader than Sameer, but the film never ranks bodies or makes strength his entire identity. His adult head-to-body relation stays consistent across standing, seated and running poses.

His most readable identifiers are a **saffron patka**, a full groomed black beard, a broad warm brown face, an original navy-and-maroon football shirt, black shorts and strong open-handed gestures. Keep the headwear correctly fitted, stable and respectfully depicted. The patka is not loosened, lost, grabbed or turned into a gag. Do not reveal or alter the hair it covers between shots. Any detailed headwear construction should be checked by someone familiar with the intended design before a final sheet is locked.

The visual language is original cinematic 3D feature animation, with soft believable cloth/skin shading and expressive adult features. There is no actor likeness or real football-club kit. Shoes, bag, bottle and ball are unbranded. The original club-free striped shirt is sufficient recognition; do not add a familiar sponsor word or badge.

## Face construction

- **Head shape:** a broad rounded rectangle softened at temples and cheeks. Cheekbones are present but do not make the face hollow. Beard extends the lower silhouette and should not be mistaken for the true chin becoming longer in profile.
- **Forehead and patka line:** enough forehead remains visible for brow movement; the saffron cloth follows the same neat edge every time. The knot/raised contour remains consistent between front, three-quarter and back. Do not add a turban, cap or a different cloth fold in only one angle.
- **Brows:** thick, dark, mostly horizontal with a natural rise near the outer third. They can lift in an inviting question, draw together during concentration and release during laughter. Do not keep a comic angry scowl as a shorthand for a sports player.
- **Eyes:** dark-brown irises, warm gaze, moderately large in the chosen 3D style. A focused kick uses narrowed lids, then eyes track the ball before a head turn. At a table, he looks at the person speaking rather than constantly addressing the viewer.
- **Nose:** medium-to-broad bridge and a softly defined rounded tip, connected naturally to the cheeks. The nose stays the same under sweat and different light. No hooked caricature or red clown-like nose.
- **Mouth:** expressive lips visible within the beard boundary, enough movement for a small question and a spontaneous laugh. The moustache must not hide every phoneme or float independently. Smile corners lift the cheeks and narrow the eyes; teeth appear briefly, not as a permanent row.
- **Beard:** a full black beard with a neat rounded lower outline, soft cheek transition, short sideburn connection and moustache following the upper lip. Keep its length and shape identical through the two-Sunday story. Separate large volume shapes from a little surface hair texture; avoid hundreds of visibly moving strands. It follows the jaw/head and compresses slightly against a raised chin, never detaches or clips into the neck.
- **Ears:** mostly visible as appropriate to the locked headwear/sideburn arrangement; perspective changes their visible size, not their placement. No new ear jewellery appears after the game.

## Wardrobe K1 — first Sunday game

- Original navy-and-maroon broad vertical stripes on a matte breathable short-sleeved shirt. Keep stripe count and placement locked: the central panel and shoulder line should not swap colors after a reverse angle. A plain dark ribbed crew neck sits below the neck, never a branded V-neck or another team's exact collar.
- Simple raglan or set-in seams chosen once on the final sheet. Sleeves finish around the middle of the upper arm and move with shoulder rotation. The hem remains outside the shorts unless the owner chooses a consistent tuck.
- Black sports shorts, comfortable mid-thigh-to-above-knee length, with plain waistband and drawcord not usually visible. No large dangling cord near feet. Side split is modest and consistent, and shorts do not turn into long trousers at the eatery.
- Charcoal sport socks and plain turf shoes with a small textured sole. Shoes have no stripe/swoosh/wordmark, and soles contact level synthetic turf. Do not render long metal studs on a hard walkway or in the café.
- Saffron patka remains neat and fitted through exercise. A soft cream towel is in his bag or hand, not wrapped over headwear to conceal a design drift. He wipes the lower cheek/neck, not repeatedly pulls at the patka.
- An optional simple kara can be included only if deliberately locked on the character sheet and handled consistently. It is not required by this story, and adding or removing it between shots is an error. If included, ensure it does not become a watch, multiple bangles or a comedy prop.

## Wardrobe K2 — first Sunday food stop

This is the same shirt and shorts after the match, with towel used briefly and then parked on the bag. In a clear bench/locker cut he can change from turf shoes to **plain dark walking trainers** carried in the bag. Record which shoe state each shot uses; don't invent a full shower/makeover in an invisible transition. He drinks plain water while resting, not boiling chai while running. A small sports duffel holds the used shoes in a separate cloth compartment. The football is left secured at the pitch or placed in the bag, not brought onto a crowded food table as a rolling hazard.

## Wardrobe K3 — next Sunday coda

The same washed kit and same fitted headwear establish recurring identity. It is not the same sweaty afternoon continuing without a time jump. Show a “Next Sunday” edited cue or an obvious new-day establishing cut. Socks/kit colors remain identical even though the shirt is clean. Do not add a captain's armband, numbered professional uniform or coaching whistle as a sudden status reward. He is inviting people into a casual game, not becoming a professional coach because of one meetup.

## Hands, posture and movement

Gary uses grounded movement: a short approach to the ball, planted support foot, hip rotation and a controlled follow-through. For complex sport contact, the shot can cut at foot impact and separately show the ball outcome. Do not ask one take to invent every limb, a long camera orbit, the goalkeeper and a flying ball simultaneously. His gaze finds the target before he moves; shoulders counterbalance the leg action. After exercise, breath and shoulder settling are visible without theatrical gasping.

At the boundary bench, knees have enough space and shoes stay flat. A water-bottle cap is unscrewed once, held or parked, then replaced; no duplicate cap. At the table he puts a cup down before a big gesture. The safe tactics joke uses a **folded napkin as a tiny diagram/ball marker**, pushed a few centimeters with a fingertip. He does not kick a momo at friends, scatter hot drinks or stand on a chair. The comedy is his enthusiasm making a miniature play look important.

## Attitude through behavior, not labels

When the old teammates must leave, Gary can want another few minutes without accusing them of being bad friends. He returns a wave and a good-natured “Theek hai, milte hain.” He sits to cool down, notices a food-and-chat possibility and makes a concrete low-pressure invitation. He offers an empty chair and asks if a newcomer wants to join the next casual session. The newcomer is free to say maybe. The film does not require everyone to become loud or show enthusiasm in the same way.

He pays attention when another person talks. His big gestures pause, his mouth closes and his eyes follow their example. His warmth is in the invitation and the room he makes around the table, not in a permanently shouting stereotype. A Hindi base with light Punjabi warmth can be discussed for voice, but no exaggerated catchphrase, religious gag or one-note accent is needed.

## Eight expression keys

| Key | Face | Body/action |
|---|---|---|
| Aiming | Lower lids narrow, brows settle, lips closed | Head still, torso balanced over planted foot |
| Contact effort | Brief compressed lips, gaze stays on action | Hip/shoulder counterrotation, grounded ankle |
| Catching breath | Mouth slightly open, brows relaxed | Shoulders rise/fall, hands settle on hips briefly |
| Good-natured question | One brow lifts, half-smile | Palm opens at waist, not a pointing accusation |
| Thinking | Eyes look toward phone then real space | Bottle set down, torso leans a little forward |
| Greeting | Small eye narrowing and easy smile | One short wave, no forced hug |
| Explaining a play | Focus shifts between napkin and listener | Finger moves one marker; free hand stays clear of cups |
| Shared laugh | Cheeks lift, eyes narrow, beard follows jaw | Shoulders move, body remains safely seated |

## Five signature poses

1. Ready-to-pass stance: knees soft, feet spaced, weight balanced, hands loose, not permanently clenched fists.
2. Post-game boundary rest: feet grounded, towel over one bag strap, bottle in one hand, breathing slowing.
3. Friendly departure wave to an old teammate, shoulders relaxed rather than slumped or accusatory.
4. Table welcome: one hand indicates the empty chair while the other remains safely beside his parked glass.
5. Mini tactics demonstration: seated, index finger moves one folded napkin, eyes check whether the other person follows, small laugh when they improve his plan.
