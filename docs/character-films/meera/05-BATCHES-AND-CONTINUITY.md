# Meera — editable batch and continuity plan

**160 seconds / 32 shots / six independent scene batches.** A batch is an editorial package, not a command to render twenty-five seconds in one unreviewed request. Each shot retains its own prompt, accepted take, voice line and selected edit range. Start with one representative test, review it, then fill the batch. Keep good originals when changing a neighboring beat.

## Preflight / design batch D00 — not part of the 160-second runtime

Prepare base Meera front/three-quarter/profile/back and the eight expressions in the character bible. Fix her adult proportions before costume work. Then create consistent PR-01, ATH-01 and ST-01 wardrobe states and the two simple original supporting adults. Do not ask a generation to choose random pieces from all three clothes lists. A dancer-reviewed stage/costume reference is needed before treating ME22/ME27–ME29 as technically correct movement.

Prepare neutral prop references: racket/grip, one shuttle, bottle, phone edge/back, folded programme and stage bells. Make small room/court/hall geography maps sufficient to keep eyelines and entrances consistent. There is no need for a full architectural model. Exact text stays a replaceable composed element. No original moodboard photograph or actual participant identity is required.

The voice sample set can be prepared independently when authorized access and a selected voice exist. Keep speech timing flexible until then. A face performance without final sound remains a visual test, not finished lip sync.

## B01 — input, handoff and acceptance

**Start:** PR-01, low bun, clean practice floor, no stage bells. **End:** ATH-01 on a court bench, racket across thighs, capped bottle beside bag, attention turning toward phone. The change from practice to court requires an explicit day cut; it is not a continuous outfit transformation.

**Assets:** practice frame and simple movement reference; ATH-01 standing/bench reference; racket/shuttle; court layout. **First test:** ME04's single wall return or ME01's reviewed step. Check feet/floor contact and adult scale. If a shuttle path fails, fix that small take, not the face in all five shots. Dialogue ME-L01 can be timed after the main body movement.

**Accept when:** the face remains identifiable between practice and sport, the hem/feet behave like fabric/body, only one shuttle exists and her question is ordinary self-talk. Reject a frozen portrait, sudden costume morph or generic sorrow montage.

## B02 — input, handoff and acceptance

**Start:** phone pickup/planning from the B01 state. **End:** first-Sunday greeting at the safe boundary, rackets lowered. **Assets:** plain phone geometry/blank insert, original wish/logistics design, Nisha/Aarav adult references and first-meeting blocking.

**First test:** ME10 with two characters and one small wave. Keep extra people simple; no six-lead crowd is needed. An accepted phone performance can be reused beneath revised UI. The later reply should have a different day cue, not immediate instant attendance. Slots for public court/date/start/end/full cost/organizer stay placeholders until real details exist.

**Accept when:** the phone stays singular, other people have choice/time, a wish is not confused with a court booking, and the first hello does not place bags or people in an active playing line. Check the supporting voice is distinct and the exact ME-L02–ME-L05 words remain intact.

## B03 — input, handoff and acceptance

**Start:** partners ready for their first gentle rally. **End:** third-Sunday table, folded programme on Meera's lap. **Assets:** two-player court staging, same bag/racket/bottle, cups/plate, programme and three day-state references.

**First test:** ME11's one-step hesitation and safe stop. A missed shuttle is enough for comedy; no collision or dangerous racket swing. The chai scene is a separate location/time cut and cups must be set down before large gestures. ME14 and ME15 need explicit second/third-Sunday cues so familiarity is earned.

**Accept when:** each rally path/contact is readable, identity/clothes do not drift between weeks, the same two friends stay recognizable and the programme comes from her own bag. The week labels are edit assets that can change without rerendering her face. ME-L06–ME-L09 are short distinct voices; do not add a narrator explaining trust.

## B04 — input, handoff and acceptance

**Start:** same third-Sunday table with programme. **End:** performance-day backstage arrival in covered ST-01, bag safely set down. **Assets:** offered programme, invitation insert, PR-01 mirror and dancer-reviewed ST-01 fit/back view, shawl/plain flats, hall entrance.

**First test:** ME17's programme offer: one hand turns it, another adult looks, no object fusion. The scene needs space after ME-L10 and a genuine interested-but-conditional ME-L11. Do not replace it with an attendance guarantee. Practice and performance-day changes are deliberate edits, not magical dressing.

**Accept when:** Nisha's response preserves choice, the phone/card contains no actual unsupplied event data, the mirror is coherent and Meera has a life beyond waiting for replies. Stage face should match the base adult face under makeup. If costume approval changes, only the ST-01 parts of this batch and all later ST-01 scenes need visual revision; the badminton footage can remain.

## B05 — input, handoff and acceptance

**Start:** prepared ST-01, secured costume, backstage bag put away. **End:** first short dance phrase resolved in a balanced position. **Assets:** reviewed costume/ankle straps, stage mirror, stage-wing eyeline, auditorium seats, friends' stable casual outfits and movement reference.

**First test:** ME27, with enough camera distance to inspect head/arms/feet and a short coherent phrase. Do not invest in many decorative stage angles before joint/floor/fabric contact works. ME22 can show a fastening check rather than complex knot construction. ME23's exhale is a shoulder action, not just a whispered sound pasted onto a frozen face.

**Accept when:** stage costume/skin/face stay stable, no random hand symbols or unsupported extreme poses appear, friends settle before the excerpt, and a tiny recognition smile leads back into the dance. Noise-makers, banners, sports slogans and loud mid-performance cheering fail this brief. ME-L14 is a low aside to a seated friend, not dialogue projected at Meera.

## B06 — input, handoff and acceptance

**Start:** same performance excerpt/lighting and ST-01 state. **End:** later tea scene and a readable original end card. **Assets:** second movement/expression reference, final hold, ordinary audience applause, later shawl/flats table frame and final text design.

**First test:** ME29's resolved hold followed by appropriate applause timing, or ME31's natural cup set-down/line. Do not let crowd audio begin before the movement finishes. The performance is a selected excerpt; the cut may bridge sections, but not physically impossible poses. Keep background faces modest and original.

**Accept when:** Meera's phrase actually resolves, Aarav's line is short and after the ending, tea occurs at a clear later time, and the final suggestion sounds like a plan to consider. No exact Sunday/court booking becomes a real public claim. If closing text needs more than the remaining seconds, extend the final graphic hold instead of speeding a sentence.

## Runtime options without losing the story

**Base:** 160 seconds. **Longer, approximately 175–180 seconds:** add five seconds to the dance excerpt, five to an early first greeting/rally, and five to the tea/voice pause; do not pad with a motionless face. **Shorter, approximately 135–145 seconds:** combine one of ME14/ME15's week cues, reduce the programme detail and shorten a location establishment. Retain a visible repeated-meetings cue, the personal invitation, an actual dance fragment and applause after its finish. Any edit map must be recalculated; never keep stale timecodes while saying the new cut is locked.

Save changes as story/design/voice/edit versions. A review note should state the exact shot and problem—such as “ME22 strap enters skin” or “ME25 arrivals too loud”—rather than asking for an entire new film with every existing good detail at risk.
