# Meera — character and continuity bible

**Working design ME-C01 · Adult, 24 · Original cinematic 3D animation proposal.** This is a reproducible written design, not an approved generated model sheet. The renderer may soften materials and eyes, but it must not make her a child or borrow a recognizable actor's face. Tara's different approved face/hair/body are not a shortcut for Meera.

## Person, wants and attitude expressed through behavior

Meera has a practice schedule, a job/studies outside this film that need not be invented, an interest in badminton and an existing dance life. She wants a few people with whom it is easy to play, have chai and stay in touch. Sharing her performance invitation feels a little more personal than suggesting a game. That small step supplies the emotional arc. She is not rescued from having no identity or suddenly rewarded for becoming more extroverted.

At the court she puts her bottle away from the playing line, checks her partner's side before serving and collects a stray shuttle before continuing a conversation. When she misses a shot she looks at the shuttle, then at her partner and smiles before finding the next one. When someone else misses, she makes the same calm reset. These repeated behaviors show her attitude more clearly than a personality label.

During practice she marks a difficult section slowly rather than repeating it at full speed. She taps the edge of a folded programme against her palm before offering it, then lets the recipient decide. Backstage she presses both feet into the floor, releases one long breath and lowers her shoulders. She does not become a different person onstage; the same precise attention becomes easier to see.

## Face structure — front, three-quarter and profile

**Overall:** a softly oval face, slightly wider through the cheekbones than at the jaw. The jaw tapers gently to a small rounded chin; it is not a sharply pointed generic anime V. Cheeks have natural fullness when smiling, without a different rounder face for comedy.

**Forehead and brows:** medium-height forehead; dark full brows, mostly straight through the inner two-thirds with a modest lift toward the outer third. Preserve the distance between brows and eyes when she becomes expressive. Brows do not turn thin or floating in performance makeup.

**Eyes:** dark brown irises with a warm small highlight, almond-shaped lid outline and a little lower-lid roundness. The eyes can enlarge slightly for readability in the selected 3D treatment, but retain adult spacing and cheek structure. Looking down at a shuttle is a real eye-line change, not a full face redesign. Avoid permanently huge shining eyes or constant tears.

**Nose:** medium-width base, gentle bridge visible in profile, rounded tip and natural nostril wings. Keep nose length/base consistent between front and profile; do not replace it with a tiny upturned button in beauty shots.

**Mouth:** medium width, a soft upper cupid's bow and a slightly fuller lower lip. Resting lips close naturally without a fixed smile. Her right mouth corner lifts a little earlier in an amused smile, producing a small cheek crease rather than a new deep dimple. Character-right means her own right, not the viewer's right.

**Skin:** warm medium-brown base, never porcelain. Working color-key targets under neutral light: base `#9A654A`, warm highlight `#C18B67`, shadow `#6B493B`, lip `#8B4B44`. These are material/color targets, not instructions to paint flat swatches on a face. Daylight, court lamps and stage light may change illumination while preserving the underlying skin tone.

**Small details:** no required nose stud, permanent bindi, scar or beauty mark is invented as an identity anchor. A small central stage bindi belongs to the stage makeup state. The face must remain recognizable without makeup or jewelry.

## Eight expression keys

1. **Practice attention:** relaxed lower face, brows gently collected, gaze directed at her own movement.
2. **Missed-shot recognition:** eyelids blink once, lips press briefly, then a side smile as she looks at the fallen shuttle.
3. **Invitation hesitation:** programme tapped once against palm, eyes lift to the friend and look away for a small breath.
4. **Listening:** mouth rests closed; small head angle and eye tracking, not a constant nod or smile.
5. **Friendly correction:** eyebrows lift and right mouth corner turns, as if proposing another try rather than scolding.
6. **Backstage uncertainty:** a controlled exhale, slightly tightened lips, hand settles on garment edge; no tears or extreme fear.
7. **Recognition in audience:** eyes focus on familiar people, brow releases, one small smile appears without breaking the performance.
8. **After-performance pleasure:** cheeks lift, shoulders release, a laugh interrupts a sentence naturally. Same face, not a glamorized celebratory version.

## Five signature poses

1. Racket resting across thighs, forearms relaxed, gaze from shuttle to the empty partner space.
2. Feet reset shoulder-width after a missed rally; left hand makes a small “again?” gesture while right holds racket down.
3. Seated over chai, one elbow close to body and programme offered across the table with an open palm.
4. Backstage both feet grounded, fingers resting lightly together near waist, shoulders dropping with the exhale.
5. Foyer bag strap steadied with one hand while the other holds her own cup; head turns to a friend's next-game suggestion.
