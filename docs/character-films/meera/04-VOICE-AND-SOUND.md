## The lead's voice — ME-VOICE-01

Proposed pitch is a medium to gently lower female register with a clear center, a little natural grain and supported rather than constantly airy breathing. Dance practice can inform her steady breath; casual conversation is not a dramatic classical-stage recitation. Avoid a tiny cartoon-girl pitch, constant smile, generic ad voice or actor imitation.

Her exact home language has not been established. Do not invent a Tamil accent from her dance training or a Gujarati caricature from the city. Use natural Indian Hindi pronunciation and familiar English words inside it. Keep “badminton”, “beginners”, “performance” and “audience” conversational instead of exaggerated English-accent demonstrations. An actual speaker's performance is more useful than a stereotype.

Working pace is about **120–140 words per minute**, with 0.2–0.5-second thinking/listening spaces. This is an acting target, not an API value. A rally call can be quicker; private backstage self-talk can slow down. If a chosen voice needs more time, extend the edit. Do not accelerate every line to protect a provisional 160-second target.

Her opening wish is practical, lightly amused. The personal invitation has one small preparatory breath, then clear words. Backstage the volume is low and the exhale real. Afterward her voice has a released breath and a smile rather than a grand speech about her transformation. The same vocal identity survives all these states.

## Performance and mouth planning

Prepare each line separately with its ID as the filename stem. Keep a clean performance, an alternate with slightly more/less energy and any breath/reaction needed around it. Do not feed “Meera says”, the ID, bracketed directions or both language versions into the spoken text. Words, breath and silence should remain distinguishable assets.

Actual recordings determine cue starts, word boundaries and mouth keys. Do not claim phoneme accuracy from this table. Let the body begin the action before or around the sentence rather than adding endless mouth motion over a frozen face. Listening faces blink/breathe without speaking. ME-L06 has a real hesitation gap; ME-L13 rides the exhale; ME-L16 keeps its consonants soft but intelligible beneath the room sound.

During the dance excerpt Meera does **not** lip-sync an invented song. Mouth expression follows the prepared performance reference. If a final accompaniment includes sung words, its lyric/choreography relationship needs its own coherent plan. A visible speech take can use ordinary mouth closures for m/b/p sounds, but a rigid frame chart should wait until actual voice exists.
